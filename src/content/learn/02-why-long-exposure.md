A normal photograph freezes an instant. A long exposure records a *duration* — and duration is where the magic hides.

Water turns to silk because thirty seconds of ripples average into smoothness. Clouds streak into brushstrokes. Stars, which your eye sees as fixed, reveal that the whole sky is turning. And a person walking through the frame with a light source becomes a glowing line with no person visible at all — that's light painting, and it's why a flashlight belongs in every camera bag.

The technique asks for three things: a tripod (non-negotiable — the camera cannot move even a hair), patience, and darkness. The camera sees cumulatively; you see instantaneously. Learning long exposure is learning to predict what accumulated time looks like.

Noise is the tax on long exposures. Sensors warm up, and hot pixels bloom into fake stars. Serious night shooters capture *dark frames* — same settings, lens cap on — and subtract them, which is exactly what the Twilightscapes camera's dark-frame tool automates.

Every photograph in this app carries its recipe — open any photo's info panel and you'll see the exposure that made it. Steal those numbers freely. That's what they're there for.
